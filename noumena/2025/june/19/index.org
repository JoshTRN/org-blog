* Good Morning, Baby Girl

I didn't talk much with Noumena this morning. Sadly, I don't even recall hugging her before she left for daycare.
-----

* Noumena Gets Home Early!

Noumena rushes into the house as Mommy brought her home early. From my office, I hear her singing about how she's very special and very happy. A smile overcomes my face as I listen finishing up work. There's nothing better a person can hear from their child. Despite this and shortly there after, she's complaining that "Kowie's an asshole."

Kowie and Noumena's conflict escalates until she's screaming "Fuck you!" repeatedly at Kowie. We've taught Noumena that it's inappropriate to say that to us as there's a level of respect we command as parents and moreover that respect needs to be extended to other people. But, in her conflicts with Kowie, I see this as basically harmless and it clearly feels very expressive to her, so I don't mind.

In this case, however, I'm concerned that Noumena may be saying "Fuck you" to her mother so I get up from my desk to investigate. Upon realizing that the conflict is strictly between her and Kowie, my concern is relaxed. Mommy tells Noumena to just disengage with Kowie if Kowie isn't treating her how she wants, which only serves to further upset Noumena. She approaches Mommy, upset and wanting to do something about it gesturing at striking Mommy. Seeing this, I interpret what Noumena's upset about to be unsolicited advice... so I prompt her to solicit it, asking "Do you want suggestions on how to handle Kowie?" She says yes and so I just re-explain what her mother had told her which she receives well, effectively solving the problem.

Noumena, having calmed down, runs up to greet me, hugging my legs and doing a number of back flips pushing her legs off of my body as I hold her arms suspending her to give her a rotational axis. I kiss her and inform her that I have to get back to work and take my leave to my office.

* Going Outside

Once I'm done working, I go back into our bedroom to greet Noumena. She's both excited to tell me about the cardinal that visited the transparent bird feeder affixed to our window and disappointed that I didn't see it. She wants to go outside to go see if we can see it. I tell her I'm going to the restroom and then we can go outside. She follows me in bringing her shoes with her to put them on explaining that we only need to find a single cardinal. I ask her if we can see multiple and she insists on only seeing "one cardinal" holding up her left index finger, "not two." I'm curious why she's so adamant that it must be a single cardinal and ask her "would it be wrong if we found two?" She never answers and we head out the door.

We never see any cardinals outside, but a snail scaling the backside of my house below her eye level catches my eye. Perplexingly, despite its obviousness, she insists that she can't see the snail. As I try to work out what she's truly trying to communicate, I'm being swarmed by mosquitoes. I tell Noumena about the mosquitoes to get her to follow me out into the broader yard to avoid them. We've traveled to the side of the house and not entirely avoided the mosquitoes. I kill several biting my legs and when one lands on her she expects me to kill it as well. I tell her to smack it! We have to get even further away! So, we go to the street in front of our house.

I'm reminded that new wood chips were placed in the playgound near our house so I ask Noumena if she wants to go check it out and we begin walking to Tidewater Acres Park.

Noumena and I begin our play at the swings. One swing has been flung over-top the bar it's suspended from wrapping once around it and there's bird poop on its seat. I point the poop out to her as something yucky to be avoided. She asks why it's yucky and how it got there. I tell her that it's literally waste product and speculate that a bird perched on the bar the swing is suspended from when the bird had to relieve itself. I look up the contaminants found in bird poop but neglected to tell her of the plethora of bacterial contaminants contained therein.

We swing for a short while before she wants to go elsewhere.

After playing hide a seek a bit, Noumena realizes that she's thirsty. Initially, she asks for water as I go to investigate a spider web constructed on the park benches. She changes her mind to soy milk. I tell her that we have it at home and ask if she's ready to go home to get some. She affirms and we take our leave.

Cars often---and bizarrely to my mind---park on the easement that surrounds the park and today is one such case. As we exit through the gate and begin to walk around the car a woman is standing outside of the passengers-side door of the car. She greets Noumena and so I greet her back on Noumena's behalf before telling Noumena who's staring at the woman that it's okay to say "hi." Noumena waves preferring the nonverbal approach to greeting.

When we're past the car, the woman remembers that she wants to give us a pamphlet. The man who was sitting in the driver's seat then exits, telling her that he’ll get it for us. As he approaches, I realize, upon reading the cover of the pamphlet, that they're Jehovah's Witnesses.

I say, "I don't need that. I don't believe in God."

He asks, "How long have you not believed in God?"

And I respond, "Oh, since I was about 12."
Incidentally, this turns out to be false. It's closer to about age 10 when I stopped believing, and I'm not sure why 12 came to mind.

He presses his inquiry: "And what made you stop believing?"

I tell him, "I just kind of realized it's like Santa Claus."

He insists that’s not true, and I retort that I know he thinks that, but I just disagree.

Noumena still has soy milk on her mind as I carry her home. She repeats that she wants it. When she says she wants soy milk, I ask her if she thinks Daddy will give it to her. Apparently, she also had the conversation I had just had with that man in mind because instead of answering the question, she says "Not God give me soy milk."

I'm elated and so proud of her. She realizes the truth about the concrete reality without me having to instruct anything about it. I exclaim something along the lines of "Hell yeah, girl!" and insist on a high five. A smile illuminates her face as she gleefully participates in the high five. My enthusiasm has rubbed off onto her.

She continues, "Not assholes give me soy milk."

"That's right, baby girl," I respond. "Does Daddy give you soy milk?"

"Yes."

"And why's that?" I inquire.

She concludes, "Cause Daddy loves me."

We see a "family" of cats on our way home. I'd been carrying her

* Bed Time

I respect Noumena’s attempts to find loopholes to stay up later, despite the inconvenience they beset me with. When we get in bed, she says she wants water. I know what she's doing. She's taking advantage of the fact that I think it would be cruel not to give her water if she's thirsty and so have developed a tradition of giving her water before bed. I hadn't this time because she'd just drank a bunch of water not 10 minutes ago. Nevertheless, I still hadn't given it to her directly before bed time, and she knows it. She seizes this opportunity and I drag her out of bed by her arm begrudgingly yet playfully commenting on how I'm aware of her tactics.

After she's hydrated and back in bed, she tells me she wants to hear a story about the boogeyman. I despise efforts to frighten children into compliance, so I concoct a unconventional story that the boogeyman comes to visit children at night, because he's afraid of the sun. She asks why he's afraid of the sun and I say it's because he's pale and can't use sunscreen. I continue saying that---because he comes at night---children are initially afraid of him. But he's actually super nice. He does back flips to disarm children of there worries. He spins around and /always/ falls on his butt for the sake of humor. I ask her if she wants a demonstration. She does, so I get up out of her bed, spin around and fall on my butt. It doesn't inspire the laughter I'd hoped it would. Nevertheless, she's entertained.

Noumena insists that the boogeyman is mean, and I insist to the contrary---that it only seems that way because he comes at night when children can't see clearly. She accepts my revision eventually and then says she wants to watch videos.

She's doing it again... deploying her cunning tactics to delay bed time. I'm wise to them and tell her we can only watch two minutes of video and we have to go to sleep. She concedes to the time limit, and I let her select the videos in that time frame.

Noumena wants kisses in the same vein as last night. Instead, she starts counting. 1... and 1 kiss follows. 2... and 2 kisses in quick succession follow. 3...

Finally, we're at our nightly routine of me scratching her mosquito bites from the day until she rolls over and I tickle-scratch her to sleep.

Every day with you is an adventure, Noumena. Thank you for gracing my life with your insurmountable presence. I love you, honey. Sleep well.
